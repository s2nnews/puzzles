Where the money goes — net sales to contribution profit

Every dollar of the period's $6,182 net sales, drained by the real costs of doing business. What's left — contribution profit — is what pays overheads (rent, software, your time) and funds growth. Cost of goods is real: actual Shopify unit costs, GST-inclusive to match the revenue basis, on every line that carries one. Only the revenue with no cost loaded is plugged, at the modelled rate, so the assumption is confined to the gap instead of being applied to everything. The Gross margin tile above states how much of the revenue is at real cost for the range you have selected.
Bottom line: after making and shipping the puzzles, boxing them and buying the traffic, you kept about $1,223 this period — roughly $13 per order, a 19.8% contribution margin. That's the number to grow. The two biggest levers are cost of goods (load supplier costs on the other ~46% of SKUs so this stops being an estimate) and the shipping bleed (you eat ~$5.51 a parcel).

Acquisition campaigns — buying subscribers, not sales

Lead-generation performance target under $3 per subscriber

These campaigns are not trying to sell anything, so they are kept out of the ROAS table above, where a purchase-revenue denominator would score them as total failures. Judge them on cost per subscriber against the $3 target. Two counts are shown deliberately. Meta counts a lead when its pixel fires on submit; we count a subscriber that actually reached the database. When those disagree, the second is the one you can email, and the gap is the thing to fix. Numbers are a manual snapshot from Ads Manager, refreshed in leadgen.csv, because Meta's lead action is not in the Porter feed.
Quiz leads — did paid acquisition produce buyers?

Lead cohorts by acquisition source, all time

Last-click cannot answer this question. Someone clicks a Meta ad, takes the quiz, then buys three weeks later from a newsletter link, and GA4, Shopify and Omnisend all hand that sale to email. So this walks the other way: the acquisition tag is written onto the customer at signup and stays there, so every order they ever place can be traced back to what brought them in. Only orders placed after signup count. Revenue per lead is the number that sets the budget: cost per lead has to sit under it.

Organic search — the channel you do not pay for

Search Console clicks & impressions daily, all history

The only unpaid-demand line on this page. Everything above is sales, email or ads, all of which stop the moment you stop working them. Clicks are the metric. Impressions are kept because they are the denominator, not because they are an achievement. On their own impressions are vanity: Google can show the site to thousands of people at position 12 and none of it banks. What they buy you is the ability to tell two very different problems apart. Clicks down while impressions hold means the listing is being seen and not chosen, which is a title and CTR job. Clicks down with impressions is a ranking or demand job. Without the denominator, both look identical and you cannot know which one you have. That is not theory here: the single biggest prize on the site is the Ravensburger collection page at 3,691 impressions and 18 clicks, a 0.49% CTR. That opportunity is invisible without impressions, and it is worth more than any new article. This chart deliberately ignores the date picker above. The property holds nothing before 14 July 2026, so the full series is the whole record.
